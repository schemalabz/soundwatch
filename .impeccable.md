# Soundwatch — Design Context

## Product
Public noise-monitoring platform for Athens (soundwatch.gr): ~50 sensors
streaming sound levels. The dashboard lets anyone explore noise by time and
place — filter to weekends, nights, months, or areas, watch playback over
the map, or follow live levels.

## Target audience
- Curious residents of Athens: "how loud is my neighborhood, really?"
  Mobile-heavy, Greek-first (el default locale, en secondary).
- Journalists, advocates, and community groups building a case about noise —
  they screenshot and share filtered views.
- Municipal staff evaluating complaints and policy.

## Use cases
- Land on a live map, zero clicks to "what's loud right now".
- Compose filters (days × hours × months × locations) and see exactly which
  time periods are selected, then play them back like a video.
- Read honest numbers: LAeq and percentiles in device-dB (uncalibrated for
  now), with coverage honesty (no data ≠ quiet).

## Brand personality / tone
A precision instrument, not a marketing site. Clean, minimal, calm,
exceptionally polished — the feel of a beautifully machined measuring tool.
Confidence through restraint: quiet cool neutrals carry the chrome; ONE warm
accent is reserved for sound itself (levels, selection, liveness). Nothing
decorative that doesn't inform.

## Palette (configurable — single source in globals.css)
- Ink (gunmetal): #2D3142
- Slate: #4F5D75
- Silver: #BFC0C0
- Paper: #FFFFFF (tinted in practice, never pure)
- Accent (sound/live/selection): #EF8354
Light UI by default; the accent must always mean "sound activity or
selection", never decoration.

## Signature element
The time seeker bar: a thin, precise rail under the map showing the full
data range, with filter-selected periods highlighted, a fine playback
needle, and a live zone on the right (pulsing "Xs ago"). On mobile it turns
vertical and thinner. This is the element people should remember.

## Stack constraints
Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, Tailwind 4, shadcn/ui, Mapbox GL, next-intl
(el/en). Typeface must cover Greek. Mobile adapts the interface (vertical
timebar), never amputates it.
